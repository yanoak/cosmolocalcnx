id: caravans
kicker: Yunnan to Lan Na
headline: Trade is documented. A route is not.
body: Mule caravans linked Chiang Mai into the trading world of southwest China. What cannot be drawn is a single road: routes shifted with politics, water, security and season, so this layer is a corridor, not a line.
:end

headline: Tarmac, on a different alignment
body: Highway 11 reached the city from the south-east as the last section of the Superhighway. It did not pave a caravan trail; it is a separate layer of the network, drawn from [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright).
:end

id: rail
kicker: 1 January B.E. 2464
headline: The line reached Chiang Mai
body: The Northern Line arrived through the Khun Tan tunnel, 1,352 m long and eleven years in the digging. A three-month river journey became a timetable. Modern sources render the year as 1921 or 1922; the archive gives the Thai date, so we do too.
:end

id: air
kicker: 1934, then 2013
headline: The connection with no route
body: The airport opened in 1934. The city's first coworking space followed in 2013. Work now arrives from everywhere at once, and there is no line to draw for it - which is why this is where the past hands over.
:end
