## The structural change, decided 23 Sep 2026

**Scale and tense have come apart.** Until today the mapping was one view per chapter — past to the
valley, present to the circle, 2045 to the city. The Futures material is mostly *regional*: only
four of its twelve places sit inside the 5.9 × 8.2 km diorama.

So:

| Chapter | Views it uses |
|---|---|
| Past | valley |
| Present | circle |
| **Futures** | **valley *and* city** |

**The rule survives, and this is the test it passes.** A view is a timeless substrate plus a
period-bearing overlay — geology has no tense. The valley therefore appears in two chapters with
two different overlays, and that is correct rather than a violation. What is no longer true is that
*valley* means *past*: valley and city are **scales**, past and futures are **tenses**.

The rail carries the tense at the top level and a chapter sequence inside Past and Futures, which
are the two chapters that have internal sequences. Present is a single beat and does not need one.

### Chiang Dao and Mae Chaem are outside the valley

The valley field is ±60 km. **Decided: draw them as an arrow at the frame edge** — direction and
distance — rather than extending the field. Chiang Dao carries two stories and cannot simply be
dropped; the range itself does reach into frame even though the town does not.

### Ban Tawan — west, not east

The notes doc says "just east of Tha Pae". **That is wrong and is superseded.** Ban Tawan sits
**inside the city, just west of Tha Phae Gate**. The reasoning is in the meeting and is the point of
the name: *ban* is house, *tawan* is sun; Tha Phae is the eastern gate, so a shop just inside it
stands in the shadow of the sunrise. It is a small front-porch shop, not a campus.

### KAE, and the one sensitivity

KAE's headquarters is at **Wiang Kum Kam** — the old city that was built too close to the river and
abandoned after flooding. The symbolism is deliberate: *we thrive from the ruins, we return to the
ruins, we work to prevent future ruins.* Its training areas are **zones** over river and mountain
rather than points.

**The constraint is about real-world officials, not about land.** KAE is a fictional
disaster-response force with a paramilitary flavour, so its sites must not land on anything that
reads as a real government or official installation. An earlier "nothing east of the city" note is
**withdrawn** — it was a proxy for this, and the actual rule is simply avoid the faux pas.

## Precision policy — and why it differs from the past chapter

**This is a fictional worldbuilding project, so geographic precision is not required.** Approximate
placement, zones instead of pins, and invented sites are all legitimate.

Worth stating the asymmetry, because the two chapters sit one click apart and are governed by
opposite rules:

> **The past must be honest about what it does not know. The future has no such obligation, because
> nothing in it is claimed as fact.**

In the valley-as-past, a caravan route is a broad dashed corridor *because the evidence will not
support a line*. In the valley-as-futures, a drone corridor can be a soft zone simply because that
reads better. Same rendering, different reason — and the reason should be written down wherever the
code makes that choice, or a later session will flatten the two into one convention.

## The printed map is the same artefact

Decided: the newspaper's map is a **valley isometric with a city inset** — which is exactly the
Futures chapter's two zooms. The screen and the print are the same structure at different
fidelities.

Consequence: **the location list, the blurbs and the dates should be one source feeding both.** The
map brief from the meeting — roughly A4 or larger, infographic, a side legend of short
story-derived blurbs with dates — describes the screen's caption content too.

## The world, in one paragraph

Faiways is set in Chiang Mai in 2045 (2588 BE) and is the successor to an earlier fictional paper,
*Flyways*, which took its name from bird migration to describe nomad circulation — "new rivers of
opportunity". Faiways swaps the sky for the ground: a **fai** is a river weir in the northern Thai
**mueang fai** irrigation tradition, which does not hoard or block a river but slows it and shares
the flow proportionally. The question the whole issue asks is *how does a town drink from a
torrential river without drowning* — tapping global opportunity without being swept away. If
skypunk was the "cosmo", this is the groundpunk for the "local".

**Gen C** is the device that sets the date: a hundred children conceived around the Cosmo Local CNX
event in late 2026, born 2027, turning eighteen in 2045. C for Chiang Mai, for Cosmolocal, for
Children. The pieces are field notes from their formative years — explicitly *not* warnings about a
ruined world.

## The eight pieces, and what each puts on the map

1. **Journeyfolk closes applications at 5,000** — a craft apprenticeship system. Wua Lai, Ban
   Tawan. The Craft Apprentice Visa as a subclass of the Destination Thailand Visa; eligibility
   running through a named Master rather than an institution; matched community trusts that pay
   Masters a teaching stipend; a lineage mark stamped on every piece, revocable by the village. No
   Master takes more than three apprentices — the city's "principal innovation was declining to
   scale".
2. **Lanna World School, 16 years on** — Wat Ket shed, then Anusarn/San Pa Khoi. Thai national
   curriculum to M.6 plus a fourteenth IB year; trilingual by design; joint demonstration school of
   all eight Chiang Mai universities from 2034. Built to join "two halves of a city that had never
   been properly introduced".
3. **KAE — Kinetic Aid Echelon** (recruitment advert) — Wiang Kum Kam plus zones. Transpolitical
   disaster response, founded late 2020s, now the city's largest economic engine.
4. **Drone agriculture and the end of the smog** — Chiang Dao range, Lamphun. Mueang fai applied to
   the sky: pooled open-source drones built from local bamboo, solar "weirs", elected Fleet
   Dispatchers in place of Water Masters, flying time earned with chores rather than cash.
5. **The Uncareering Farm calls it quits** — Doi Saket. Eleven laid-off retreat attendees in 2026
   who stayed; the Vows of Cosmolocality — what is light travels freely, what is heavy comes from
   close to home. The farm closes in 2045 because the commons outgrew it.
6. **Baan Wat Rong Rueng, chapter 3** — Wat Umong. A serial. Waste for Merit at 18 years, the
   temple Fab Lab, the community food forest, *grow your nutrients, buy your calories*, and the
   phrase the chapter turns on: **baan, wat, rong rian — home, temple, school.**
7. **Arusha joins the Plexus** — global. The Ping Plexus of 67 Nomad-Friendly Districts, Chiang Mai
   the first; the Ping Pass and Reciprocal Passage Accords. Built on a critique worth keeping: early
   nomad mobility was "concentrated in the hands of those who could afford it", so hosts must be
   able to become guests.
8. **L-Pop** — Doi Suthep. A festival built on a ridgeline resonance mapped between 2026 and 2030
   by "slo-madic researchers".
