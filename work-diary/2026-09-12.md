## Decisions

- **The tambon is not the neighbourhood.** Wat Ket ADM3 is 6.85 km² and runs 3 km south of the
  origin, past the 4 km² hard limit and past anything a resident would call Wat Ket. Settled by
  committing the authoritative polygon *and* making the scene an explicit rectangular clip of it —
  1.50 × 2.70 km, 2.98 km², chosen with Yan from three costed options. The clip is one constant in
  one file and is meant to be argued with.
- **Geometry was never the constraint.** The plan sized the boundary against 50–100 triangles per
  building. An extruded OSM footprint actually costs about 17, so the entire tambon would be ~35k
  against a ~100k ceiling. The extent could therefore be chosen on editorial grounds. Worth
  remembering as a general lesson: the plan's own reconnaissance numbers were right and its
  inference from them was wrong, and only measuring caught it.
- **A tagged height is evidence; a synthesised one is a guess.** Separate ceilings — 150 m for
  tags, 30 m for synthesis — after finding Supalai Monte (`height=111`) clamped to 30 m.
- **Merging went in now rather than later.** Not on the plan's task list, but 1,182 individual
  meshes is 1,182 draw calls against a budget of "a few dozen". Picking survived via face-index
  ranges, so the selection UI did not regress.
- **Elevation source: SRTM 30 m** via OpenTopoData — no account, public domain, and at this
  resolution indistinguishable from Copernicus for a flood question.

- ~~What does an `add` look like mid-scrub?~~ **There is no mid-scrub.**

  design went with the slider, and it went for free.

### ~~The document's references~~ CLOSED

- ~~What happens when a re-import drops an edit's target?~~ **Snapshot and warn.** Every `remove`
  and `replace` stores `wasAt`, the target's centroid at authoring time, so a `replace` survives
  its target disappearing upstream. The import warns loudly and lists orphans rather than failing
  — it has to keep working at 2am in week two.
- ~~How do hotspots attach?~~ **`target` where an object exists, `at` where it does not.** One or
  the other. Moving an object in the editor carries its hotspot along; a hotspot about the
  riverbank or a junction gets a bare position. **Scope is two-level** — shared hotspots at scene
  level, intervention hotspots inside their scenario.
- ~~Do scenarios compose?~~ **No.** Each is an independent edit list over the same baseline. A
  shared 2045 layer would say something true and save some duplication, but it buys a composition
  order and a second place to look when something renders wrong, at a scale this project will not
  reach. Revisit at a fourth scenario.

## Design system

Started from a photograph of the cover of **PROGRESS**, 1 June 1967, a Thai magazine found in an old
house in Chiang Mai. Four-plate offset print: green masthead, yellow wordmark, rose frame, blue map
with hand-drawn leader lines to a list of school names. Written up in `docs/design-system.md`.

- **The palette is white-balanced, not as-shot.** The raw samples carried the room's white balance —
  the paper read as a warm grey at 80% lightness. Normalised against the paper as a neutral, lifted,
  then had warmth dialled back in deliberately. The warmth is a decision; the kitchen light was not.
- **Six palette values, four hue families.** That is what a four-plate job gives you. Resist a fifth.
- **The theme is not the scene document.** The scene says `kind`; the theme says what `kind` looks
  like. Same separation as `baseline` versus `edits`, applied to appearance — so a second
  neighbourhood inherits the theme for free and reskinning never touches authored content.
- **Stock versus intervention is the semantic work.** Everything on screen is 2045, so the
  distinction that matters is the city that was already there against what a scenario proposes.
  Inherited stock reads warm yellow, interventions read rose. Visible without reading a word.
- **Materials are unlit, with a three-tone ramp per role.** Chosen for modifiability rather than
  performance: with lit materials the rendered colour is the token multiplied by lighting, so
  changing a token stops having a predictable result. Unlit makes every token literally its pixel.
- **Contrast is a rule.** The raw palette fails badly as text — rose at 2.6:1, yellow at 1.1:1. UI
  text tokens are derived darker and all clear 4.5:1. Checked by a test, not by eye, because the
  audience is standing in a bright mall reading a phone at arm's length.
- **The reference photograph is gitignored.** It is a picture of a third-party artefact and carries
  camera EXIF including GPS. The derived palette is committed; the photograph is not.

Kept in reserve: the cover is itself a data visualisation — a map with leader lines fanning out to a
list. That is a ready-made hotspot treatment, and it needs no illustrator.

**The day-one question, answered:** yes — it reads as a neighbourhood rather than as boxes, and the

reads as a landmark without any art, which is the palette doing real work already.

**Two things to look at with fresh eyes tomorrow**, both visible in the first render:

- The shophouses read *flat* — footprints 20–30 m wide against 6–9 m heights is accurate for the
  vernacular, but at this zoom they look like tiles rather than buildings. Either the camera wants
  to sit lower, or the first pass of art needs to add verticality the geometry does not have.
- The ground plane's edge is a hard diamond against a page background of nearly the same colour.
  It reads as an accident rather than a choice. Either make it much larger, or make the page
  background deliberately different so the edge is intentional.

**Three engineering findings**, none of them in the code I set out to write:

- **R3F left the canvas at 300×150.** Its parent was a flex child whose height resolves in the flex
  pass, so the measurement ran before the size settled and the resize observer never fired again —
  the size never changed afterwards. An absolutely positioned parent with a definite size at first
  layout fixes it. Worth remembering for any future canvas in this project.
- **Rendering is now on demand, not every frame.** Nothing in the scene animates. Drawing at 60fps
  forever spends battery and thermal budget on nothing, and thermal throttling over an exhibition
  day is the realistic phone failure — not WebGL support.
- **React had to be pinned below 19.3**, because R3F 9.7 declares a peer range of `>=19 <19.3`.
  Pinned rather than forced past with `--legacy-peer-deps`: the cap is a compatibility statement.

**The long detour was mostly environmental.** Screenshots came back blank for a while because the
automated browser tab is `visibility: hidden`, where `requestAnimationFrame` never fires, so nothing
ever painted. Forcing a render directly gave 14 draw calls and a centre pixel of `#1F3830` —
`building.civic` shade, the temple — which confirmed the scene was correct before the tab was
foregrounded. Lesson for next time: check `document.visibilityState` before debugging a renderer.
