# Bridges across the Ping

## Context

Roads are drawn into the ground texture and water is a flat polygon a hair above it, so every road
that crosses the Ping vanishes at the bank and reappears on the other side. With both banks in the
scene since this afternoon that reads as a city with no bridges, which Chiang Mai is not: Nawarat,
Nakhon Ping, Rattanakosin, the Iron Bridge, Mengrai, the Super Highway.

Checked before writing this: OSM tags every one of them. 248 `bridge=*` highway ways fall in the
rectangle, 48 cross a water polygon in the baseline, 18 for more than 40 m. Nawarat is a
`secondary`, four lanes, 128 m with 113 over water; Loi Kroh is a `trestle`; the Iron Bridge is a
`footway`. So nothing needs placing by hand. What is missing is a *generator*: the roadmap's
Tier 2, "the local vernacular, procedural, LLM-written", applied to a bridge.

## Goal

Every `bridge=*` road that crosses water in the scene gets a deck at road width raised above the
water, a ramp down to ground beyond each abutment, low parapets, and piers wherever the span is
over water. One unlit merged mesh in the district group, from the baseline alone, no hand
placement, no new asset. Nawarat Bridge reads as Nawarat Bridge.

## Approach

**The flag travels with the road.** `BaselineRoad` gains `bridge?: true`, set by `roadsFromElements`
from `tags.bridge` (anything but `no`). The road still draws on the ground texture, which is harmless
because the water covers it there; the flag is what the generator reads.

**Crossing water is decided at draw time.** A bridge way is only built if a sample every 5 m along
it lands inside a water polygon. Viaducts over roads and the Super Highway's flyovers get no deck
this week, because a flyover with nothing under it is a different generator; "bridges across the
Ping" is the brief.

**The generator is pure and in scene metres.** `bridges.ts`: `ribbon` offsets the polyline both ways
with bounded mitres; `deckStations` extends the way by a ramp length at each end and assigns deck
top and underside heights, dropping to ground on the ramps; `pierSpots` walks the original way at
a fixed spacing and keeps the spots over water; `bridgeTriangles` turns stations and spots into a
triangle soup with per-face tones from the same `toneForNormal` the buildings use. Deck height by
road class: 7.5 m for the majors, 4.5 m for a footbridge. Heights, spacing and thickness live in
one profile table.

**Modelled from scratch, but from the data.** No `.glb`. The whole bridge is a slab, two rails and
some boxes, which is what a Ping bridge is at this scale, and the generator travels to the next
city unlike a hand-placed model. If a real profile is wanted later — Nawarat's arches — that is a
Tier 3 asset placed by an edit, not this generator.

Rejected:

- *Reading `man_made=bridge` outlines.* Not in the Overpass query, and most of these bridges do not
  carry one.
- *Ramping inside the tagged segment.* The tagged way is the span; the approaches are embankments
  beyond it. A deck that slopes down to the bank inside the span dips into the river.
- *Per-bridge hand tuning.* Everything is by class. If one bridge looks wrong, fix the rule.

## Out of scope

- Flyovers and viaducts over roads.
- Bridge outlines, arches, cables. Tier 3 assets if ever.
- Boats, weirs, the Ping's own banks.

## Outcome

Shipped 19 Sep 2026. 248 roads carry the flag, 48 cross water and get built: Nawarat (128 m, five
piers), Nakhon Ping (102 m, three), Rattanakosin (150 m, four), Mahidon's two carriageways, the
Super Highway's two, Loi Kroh's trestle, the footbridges. 3,576 triangles in 318 ms, one mesh.
23 new tests; 330 in all. The scene document only grew by the flag — 13 KB — and is byte-identical
on re-run.

Seen in the browser at street scale: Nawarat is a grey deck across the Ping with its piers in the
water and the ramps landing on the road texture on both banks; nothing floats over dry land, and
the flyovers on the Super Highway correctly do not appear. Nobody who knows the bridges has yet
said whether 6.5 m is Nawarat's height; the profile table is one line per class.

Third casing collision of the day: `bridges.ts` beside `Bridges.tsx`, hence `BridgeMesh.tsx`. The
rule is now worth stating once: a pure module is lower-case, its React component gets a different
word, never the same word capitalised.
